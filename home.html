About Blood Cells

Blood cells are the fundamental components of blood, each playing a crucial role in maintaining the body's health and function. They are primarily divided into three types: red blood cells (RBCs), white blood cells (WBCs), and platelets. Red blood cells carry oxygen from the lungs to tissues and remove carbon dioxide. White blood cells form the body’s defense system, fighting infections and foreign invaders, and are further classified into types such as neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, and eosinophils, each with specific immune functions. Platelets are small cell fragments that help stop bleeding by forming clots. Studying and identifying blood cells is essential in diagnosing various medical conditions, including anemia, infections, and blood cancers like leukemia.
